<objective>
Replace the tracer's hardcoded `MAX_ATTEMPTS=5`/`RECONCILE_AFTER_SECS=3` constants with a data-driven,
per-failure-class retry policy read from `broker-config.json`, and add the payload-retention purge pass
— pinning the exact retention window via a `checkpoint:decision` first, since purging payload bytes past
that window is a one-way, unrecoverable action (D-14).

Purpose: D-10 requires retry/backoff configurable per failure class, matching the spike's own explicit
recommendation for the real build. D-14 is rated `one-way` in CONTEXT.md — this plan is the one place in
the whole phase where a `checkpoint:decision` is required before the dependent implementation task, per
the reversibility taxonomy.
Output: `broker/src/retry.rs` (failure classification + config-driven backoff), a confirmed retention
window wired into `broker/src/config.rs`, and a purge pass in the worker tick.
</objective>

<task type="checkpoint:decision" gate="blocking">
  <name>Confirm the payload retention window (D-14, one-way)</name>
  <decision>How many days should the broker retain job payload bytes (receipt text, caja-summary text,
  raw print content) before purging them? Job/event metadata (IDs, timestamps, status, attempts, errors)
  is retained indefinitely regardless of this choice — this decision only governs the payload BLOB.</decision>
  <context>
    D-14 (CONTEXT.md) locks payload retention as short (7-30 days) but explicitly defers the exact number
    to this planning/execution step, rated `one-way`: once payload bytes are purged past the chosen
    window, they cannot be recovered — a wrong-too-short choice loses evidence needed for a real dispute
    or reconciliation; a wrong-too-long choice over-retains potentially customer/payment-adjacent text
    longer than necessary (RESEARCH.md Security Domain — Information Disclosure over-retention). 19-
    RESEARCH.md's Assumption A1 pins a provisional recommendation of 14 days (the midpoint of the
    discussed range) pending this confirmation.
  </context>
  <options>
    <option id="7">
      <name>7 days</name>
      <pros>Minimizes how long potentially sensitive receipt text sits on the local disk; fastest to
      reduce over-retention risk.</pros>
      <cons>A dispute or reconciliation raised more than a week after a sale loses the original payload
      bytes (metadata/status history still survives, just not the printed content itself).</cons>
    </option>
    <option id="14">
      <name>14 days (RESEARCH.md's provisional recommendation)</name>
      <pros>Matches a typical two-week reconciliation/dispute cadence for a small single-store operation;
      already the default wired into Plan 19-02's config.rs placeholder, so choosing this requires no
      further config change beyond confirming it.</pros>
      <cons>Slightly more retained data than the 7-day option, though still short relative to the
      metadata's indefinite retention.</cons>
    </option>
    <option id="30">
      <name>30 days</name>
      <pros>Covers a full monthly reconciliation cycle, useful if the store owner reviews receipts against
      supplier/bank statements on a monthly rhythm.</pros>
      <cons>Longest window of the three for payload text (which may include customer name and amounts) to
      sit on local disk.</cons>
    </option>
  </options>
  <resume-signal>Select 7, 14, or 30 (or state a different exact day count)</resume-signal>
</task>
